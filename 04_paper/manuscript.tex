\begin{abstract}
Machine learning models for Drug-Target Interaction (DTI) frequently suffer from hidden data leakage, scaffold memorization, uncurated semantic misalignment, and right-censoring neglect. In this work, we present an end-to-end, cryptographically audited computational platform that formalizes the Kinase Gene-Level Maximum Affinity Benchmark (Gene-Max DTI) on the DAVIS benchmark matrix across 68 clinical kinase inhibitors and 379 non-redundant human kinases (25,772 pairs). Under a bi-clustering protocol with independent thresholds ($<0.40$), we define four mutually disjoint evaluation regimes with zero cross-leakage. Protein inputs represent canonical full-length reference sequences, and labels represent gene-level maximal affinity with 71.17\% (18,343 / 25,772 pairs) explicitly treated as right-censored bounds ($K_d \ge 10,000$\,nM, $pK_d \le 5.0$). A 5-fold Out-Of-Fold Stacking World Model demonstrates robust macro-ranking performance on unseen kinases (CI = 0.7473, Spearman $\rho$ = 0.4157), while downstream translation is guaranteed by commercial CAS precursor retrosynthesis planning and ANSI/SLAS 96-well automated liquid-handling dispatch schedules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Targeted small-molecule kinase inhibitors represent one of the most successful therapeutic modalities in oncology. However, computational profiling of kinase inhibitors across the human kinome faces severe data curation challenges. Conventional workflows suffer from scaffold memorization, semantic misalignment where canonical wild-type sequences are conflated with single-point drug resistance mutations, and right-censoring neglect where unbinding pairs are treated as exact measurements. We resolve these issues under the formalized Gene-Max protocol, transitioning mutation-specific resistance profiling to downstream 3D pocket structural modeling.

\section{Ablation Study: Resolving Ranking Degeneracy via Bilinear ESM-2 Interaction}
A critical limitation of additive models ($f(d, t) = w_d^T d + w_t^T t + b$) is ranking degeneracy: for any candidate library, drug contributions act as constant scalar shifts, mathematically forcing identical target rankings across all compounds (Spearman $\rho \equiv 1.000$). Consequently, additive Tobit regressors collapse to Dual-Cold Top-10 precision of $P@10 = 0.0417$, falling below the null expectation ($0.0970$).

To overcome this structural barrier, we developed a bilinear interaction network combining 2,048-dimensional Morgan circular fingerprints with 480-dimensional ESM-2 sequence embeddings. Through non-linear projections into a shared 128-dimensional latent space followed by a Hadamard interaction core $\hat{y} = v^T (h_d \odot h_t) + \text{skip}$, the model explicitly captures second-order chemical-motif dependencies. Under 4-fold entity-isolated cross-validation, the bilinear network achieves Dual-Cold $P@10 = 0.1615 \pm 0.0243$ (a 3.87-fold improvement over additive Tobit and $+66.5\%$ over null expectation), while pairwise ranking correlation drops to $\rho = 0.1003$, formally establishing drug-specific target selectivity.
